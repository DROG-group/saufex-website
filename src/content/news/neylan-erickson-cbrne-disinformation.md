Julian H. Neylan and Timothy B. Erickson argue that accurate, timely information is the backbone of any chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear or explosive (CBRNE) response, and that disinformation now threatens it at every stage. Before an incident, manufactured distrust can undermine public compliance; during a response, social-media amplification, AI-generated deepfakes and coordinated inauthentic campaigns can obstruct operations; afterwards, a polluted information environment hampers accountability.

They make the case for prebunking, pre-established relationships between responders and communities, and technical content-authentication standards as defences. The article points to SAUFEX among initiatives "trying to build community level approaches to build resilience against these types of attacks."
