## Enhance societal resilience through all-of-society participation and institutional ethics
Enhancing societal resilience needs to be the focus for all-of-society participation in decision-making regarding information manipulation. Decision-making regarding information manipulation requires more than decentralization, including the participation of independent non-governmental experts. It also requires democratization through meaningful involvement of the general public. These two dimensions are complementary: decentralization preserves independent sources of knowledge and perspective outside government institutions, while democratization ensures that citizens' perspectives and situated knowledge can become consequential for collective decision-making.\
To maximize this impact without eroding societal resilience, institutions must adhere to a dual ethical standard:
-	The duty of responsiveness: Institutions must provide substantive, high-quality answers to public contributions. Failing to respond or relying on tokenism drains citizens' "resilience batteries," risking to stimulate learned helplessness, alienation, and nihilism - vulnerabilities that information manipulation campaigns aim to exploit. 
-	The duty of restraint: Because public participation is often aggregated rather than direct, institutions must resist the temptation to prematurely assimilate fragile human experiences into rigid administrative data points. They must carry the ethical burden of interpretation without totalizing the voices that are represented. 
## In summary
Democratic resilience is enhanced when participatory processes protect participants' autonomy, belonging, and achievement and institutions accept the full ethical weight of substantive listening and responding.
