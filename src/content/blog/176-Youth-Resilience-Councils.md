## Introduction
The currently final mechanism to support students resisting premature assimilation of external personal ontologies is the Youth Resilience Council (see: [bog post 100](https://saufex.eu/insights/100-A-history-the-Youth-Resilience-Council-concept/)), the Saufex-based addition to Interdemocracy as part of its participation layer.
## Youth Resilience Councils
Participating in a Youth Resilience Council for students might seem somewhat similar to participating in a vox pop (see: [blog post 174](https://saufex.eu/insights/174-Vox-pop/)). The major difference between the two didactic mechanisms nevertheless is that the representations that are drafted by Youth Resilience Councils are submitted to the original participants for evaluation.\
Both vox pops and Youth Resilience Councils require students to understand the perspectives expressed by the original participants. However, understanding and representation are not the same. Understanding requires exploring what others mean on the basis of their personal ontologies, rather than immediately evaluating their contributions through one's own. Representation additionally requires conveying those perspectives without replacing their meaning with one's own interpretation.\
The reflections of the original participants on the initial recommendations of the Youth Resilience Councils provide an opportunity to identify the extent to which their perspectives have been adequately represented and where improvements are needed. Athough the reflections do not necessarily establish that the representation fully captures the constituting personal ontologies, they can reveal misrepresentation, omissions, and distortions.\
As such, Youth Resilience Councils can be used as a didactic mechanism to build on earlier mechanisms to support students' abilities to move from listening without immediate reaction or judgment, through understanding external personal ontologies, towards representing them without collapsing their meaning through premature assimilation. The opportunity for those represented to evaluate their representation makes this accountability an integral part of the learning process.
