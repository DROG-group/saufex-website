## Introduction
[Blog post 141](https://saufex.eu/insights/141-Leeway-and-Levinas/) suggested that leeway may be understood as the ethical and cognitive condition under which reality can enter continuity through the Other. Interdemocracy’s specific method and format are designed to make perspectives embodied by others visible without immediately transforming them into objects of reaction, judgment, persuasion, or correction.
## The participant
- The same rules and conditions for everyone make each participant’s perspective potentially equally available as a possible source of what another person cannot see from their own position. There are no preferred positions from which reality may be presented as already more complete.
- The check-in, central thought experiment question, and check-out repeatedly invite each student to make their own experience and understanding explicit. What would otherwise remain embodied in the student without becoming accessible to others is given an opportunity to become visible.
- The possibility of declining the invitation to present one’s perspective or remain silent protects the autonomy of the perspectives being encountered. What becomes visible is not a perspective extracted under pressure, but one that the person chooses to make available.
- Open questions leave room for perspectives that cannot be anticipated by the categories built into the question.
- No interaction during perspective formulation prevents students from adapting their formulation to one another while they are still developing it. The perspective that becomes visible is therefore less immediately shaped by the expectations and reactions of the surrounding group.
- The short formulation period (90/ 180 seconds) limits the extent to which students can construct a strategically acceptable response. It creates a stronger connection between what they can genuinely formulate and what subsequently becomes available to others.
- The I-form makes the perspective explicitly locatable in the person who holds it. Rather than presenting an apparently objective proposition that others can simply accept or reject or an answer that is produced as a result of loyalty to an external group or doctrine, the student makes visible how reality appears from their subjective position.
- “Thank you” as part of the response acknowledges the grace of the other participants to take the time to listen. The contribution can therefore remain present as something to encounter rather than something that must immediately be classified as right or wrong.
- Legitimation as part of a participant answer within the thought experiment makes visible why the perspective has the form it has. The other person does not merely expose a conclusion; they expose something of the path through which that conclusion has become meaningful to them.
- A real-life example as part of a participant answer within the thought experiment makes the perspective more concretely accessible. What might otherwise remain an abstract statement can become connected to an experienced reality embodied by the person expressing it.
- Separate phases of handwriting, digitalization, and reading aloud within the thought experiment create distance between formulating a perspective and encountering the perspectives of others. This makes it less likely that one's formulation is immediately absorbed into an interpersonal exchange before it has become fully visible.
- Random speaking order exposes each participant to perspectives they did not choose to encounter. Visibility is therefore not restricted to the people one already finds interesting, familiar, agreeable, or relevant. Because participants cannot know in advance whose perspective will immediately precede or follow their own - including whether that person is perceived as an in-group or out-group member - there is also less opportunity to anticipate and strategically adapt one’s formulation to a particular participant.
- No reaction to another’s answer is crucial because immediate reaction tends to place what has been heard inside the listener’s existing continuity system: agreement, disagreement, criticism, explanation, comparison, or judgment. Suspending reaction leaves the other perspective present as something that can first be encountered in its own terms.
- Recording and making the answers accessible to the group extends this visibility beyond the moment of encounter. Perspectives that might otherwise disappear after being spoken remain available for further observation and consideration.
## The facilitator
The facilitator’s corresponding role is to protect this visibility. An evidence-informed introduction as part of the thought experiment without subjective elements establishes a common frame without supplying the perspectives that students are supposed to encounter. Observation-first rather than intervention-first prevents the facilitator from prematurely interpreting what appears. The same tone, frame, epistemic and emotional neutrality, questions, instructions, and cues for everyone prevent the facilitator from selectively amplifying some perspectives over others. Also the facilitator’s “thank you” acknowledges rather than evaluates.
## Conclusion
The individual elements of Interdemocracy work in the same direction: they allow something that is embodied in another person - but normally remains invisible from within one’s own continuity system - to become perceptible without requiring that person’s ontology to be immediately translated into one's own. The format does not provide the missing reality. It creates conditions of leeway in which another person can make aspects of that reality visible.
