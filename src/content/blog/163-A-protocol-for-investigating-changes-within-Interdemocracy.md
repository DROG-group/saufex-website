 ## The protocol
Interdemocracy brings together different personal ontologies through a process of independent answering, listening, encountering recommendations, and reflecting. But how can we investigate whether this process is followed by change, and whether any such change constitutes correction?\
The starting point is not to assume that change has occurred, nor to treat every difference as correction. Instead, a protocol is needed. Since anonymity is key to Interdemocracy, the protocol can only be employed at the collective level.\
Although participants do not formally provide answers before and after the process, they engage with a topic twice: first, by answering a question, and subsequently, by reflecting on a recommendation. The protocol can therefore compare these two instances: the body of answers and the body of reflections.\
To investigate potential change and, where applicable, correction at the collective level, the proposed methodology is to establish whether the collective reflections contain elements that cannot be explained by the collective answers.
## Protocol step 1 - establish the baseline through the body of answers
Participants independently answer a question within the Interdemocracy method and format. Together, these answers constitute the collective baseline. The body of answers provides evidence of the concepts participants treat as relevant and the relations they articulate among them. It does not provide a complete representation of their personal ontologies, but establishes the material against which subsequent reflections can be examined.
## Protocol step 2 - the Interdemocracy participation process
Participants listen to one another's answers without discussion. At a later stage, they are presented with recommendations based on the complete body of answers and invited to reflect on these recommendations independently.\
The resulting reflections constitute a second body of participant perspectives. As with the initial answers, these reflections provide evidence of participants' expressed understanding, without necessarily representing the full extent of their personal ontologies.
## Protocol step 3 - compare the body of answers with the body of reflections
The two bodies of participant perspectives can now be compared. The central question is: Can the body of reflections be explained by the body of answers, or does it contain elements that the answers cannot explain?\
The comparison examines what is retained, what is reformulated, what becomes less prominent, and what appears that cannot be accounted for by the initial material.\
An element absent from the initial answers is not necessarily evidence of change. It may have been implicit in them, or it may become articulable only during reflection. The analysis must therefore examine the explanatory relations between the two bodies of material rather than treating textual novelty as sufficient evidence.\
Elements that cannot be explained by the initial answers provide a basis for investigating possible change. The comparison alone, however, does not establish that participants' personal ontologies have changed. It identifies potential change in the collective body of perspectives, which requires further examination before conclusions can be drawn about individual understanding or correction.
## Protocol step 4 - identify indications of change
Elements in the reflections that cannot be explained by the initial answers constitute indications of change in the collective body of perspectives.\
These elements can then be examined using established qualitative methods, such as thematic analysis, qualitative content analysis, or interpretive analysis. The choice of method should follow the research question and the material.\
The analysis can investigate whether the indications concern:
- Conceptual relevance: concepts that become relevant or lose relevance.
- Conceptual relations: changes in how concepts are related, even when the concepts themselves remain present.
- Other dimensions of understanding: distinctions, qualifications, or perspectives that cannot be accounted for by the initial material.

These remain indications, not proof that individual participants' personal ontologies have changed. The initial answers are an explanatory baseline, not an exhaustive record of what participants could think or articulate. Nor does a change in the collective body necessarily imply that every participant, or any particular participant, has changed their understanding.
## Protocol step 5 - investigate indications of correction
Where the collective analysis identifies indications of change, individual follow-up conversations can investigate their significance.\
These conversations can explore how participants understand the relevant differences, whether they recognize a change in their own thinking, and how they explain the relationship between their initial answers and subsequent reflections.\
The conversations should remain open to different interpretations. Participants may describe a changed understanding, clarify something that was already implicit in their initial answer, reject an interpretation of their reflection, or remain uncertain about what has changed.\
Participants' accounts provide additional evidence, but retrospective accounts cannot conclusively establish either the nature or the cause of a change. Their interpretation must take into account potential biases, including consistency bias, which may influence how participants reconstruct or describe their earlier and current positions. Nor does a participant's recognition of change, by itself, establish that the change constitutes correction.\
The outcome is therefore an indication of correction, not proof of correction. Whether a change constitutes correction remains a question for further theoretical and empirical investigation.
## What the protocol establishes
The protocol distinguishes three levels of inquiry:
- Indications of change: The collective reflections contain elements that cannot be explained by the collective answers.
- Indications of individual change: Follow-up conversations provide further evidence about whether and how these collective differences correspond to changes in participants' understanding.
- Indications of correction: Further examination provides grounds for investigating whether identified changes may be corrective.

The protocol does not presuppose that Interdemocracy produces change, that change necessarily constitutes correction, or that participants must converge on a common position. Its purpose is to make these questions empirically investigable while preserving the distinction between what the material shows, what can reasonably be inferred, and what remains unresolved.
