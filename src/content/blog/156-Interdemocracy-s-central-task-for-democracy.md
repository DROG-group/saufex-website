## 1
Autopoiesis establishes the need for otherness, not specifically for Others. Because no personal ontology can fully correct itself, self-correction requires access to what exceeds it. That can come from experiences as well as Other people.
## 2
This creates a possible libertarian fork. One could conclude simply: I need freedom to encounter experiences that expand or correct me. That would produce a kind of epistemic libertarianism - maximal freedom to experience otherness. 
## 3
Others have a distinctive epistemic value. An experience gives access to difference, whereas an Other gives access to an already organized difference: another person has constituted experience into a distinctive relational organization of concepts. Others are therefore unusually rich sources of otherness. 
## 4
But privileging Others cannot simply be justified metaphysically. Saying “the Other is intrinsically privileged” would introduce an external premise. Calling this merely “humanistic” risks hiding that move. 
## 5
The utilitarian basis is real. Initially, the reason to preserve Others as autonomous sources can be understood instrumentally: my self-correction benefits from their independently constituted perspectives. This is consistent with Interdemocracy's claim that ethics and utilitarianism can meet. 
## 6
This gives rise to responsibility for the Other. If my capacity for self-correction depends upon Others remaining autonomous sources of otherness, then my interest in my own autopoiesis gives me reason to preserve their capacity to constitute and express their own ontologies. Responsibility for the Other is therefore not opposed to self-interest: it emerges from the recognition that my continued self-correction depends upon the autonomy of Others.
## 7
Leeway and ma provide a condition for addressing the deeper problem. Both experiences and Others can be immediately assimilated into one's existing ontology. Leeway interrupts that process; ma provides an interval in which difference can remain genuinely different. 
## 8
Thus the more precise sequence is: personal ontology → autonomy → necessary incompleteness → autopoiesis → need for otherness → experiences and/or Others as sources of otherness → leeway → ma → preservation of difference → possible self-correction.
## 9
If democratic society is to remain collectively capable of self-correction, it must preserve multiple autonomous sources of perspective rather than allowing one organization of reality to become epistemically dominant.
## 10
Within Interdemocracy, democracy is therefore understood as a solution to the problem of preserving this plurality of autonomous sources of otherness. Its task is to maintain conditions in which different autonomous organizations of reality can remain sufficiently distinct to become consequential for one another.
## 11
Democracy in its current form is not the only possible solution to this problem. According to Interdemocracy, however, preserving plurality is the central task assigned to democracy.
