## 1. Personal ontology 
Each person constitutes reality through a distinctive relational organization of concepts. This organization is necessary for orientation and action, but it is necessarily limited: no personal ontology exhausts reality, nor can it be certain that it contains all relevant concepts or that the relations it establishes among them are correct.
## 2. Autonomy 
It is a basic human need to not only constitute but also to articulate one’s own personal ontology. 
## 3. Autopoiesis
Because one’s ontology is limited, one cannot fully correct it from within itself. One’s capacity for continued self-constitution and self-correction depends upon new experiences and access to what other ontologies make visible.
## 4. Leeway 
In order for external, autonomous personal ontologies to become genuinely accessible, one needs a capacity for experiencing them without immediately absorbing them into one’s personal ontology through judgment, reaction, persuasion, or conformity. One’s self-continuation processes need to be paused in order to allow self-correction to initiate.
## 5. Ma 
Leeway creates the possibility of experiencing ma: an interval in which another relational organization can be encountered rather than immediately evaluated. The encounter can or cannot lead to auto-correction.
## 6. Responsibility for the Other
Because one’s capacity for self-correction depends upon access to what other autonomous ontologies make visible, it is in one’s autopoietic interest to remain receptive to the Other - another individual authentically expressing their personal ontology. Responsibility for the Other emerges when the Other is recognized not merely as an object of one’s ontology, but as a source of possible self-correction alongside new experiences that escape immediate absorption into one’s personal ontology. Responsibility for the Other is an important aspect of being responsible for one’s own self-correction.
## 7. Belonging 
It is a basic human need to be seen, heard, and accepted by Others. The active exercise of leeway by Others, through ma and responsibility for the Other, provides a fulfilment of this need.
## 8. Ethics and utilitarianism meet
Autopoiesis provides the bridge: responsibility toward Others is not opposed to the interest of the self since the self’s capacity for correction co-depends upon Others. Under the right circumstances, responsibility for the self and responsibility for the Other meet, and a third basic human need, that of achievement, is fulfilled.
## 9. Interdemocracy
Interdemocracy provides at least some of these circumstances. Its basic layer increases the chances of autonomous constitution and ma, and, as a result, potential subsequent self-correction. Its participation layer increases the chances that encounters with Others become consequential by connecting autonomous personal ontologies to collective reflection and action.
## In summary
- Autonomy: I constitute myself.
- Autopoiesis: I cannot fully correct myself.
- Leeway / ma: I become capable of encountering what exceeds me.
- Responsibility: I remain receptive to Others and new experiences.
- Belonging: Others remain receptive to me.
- Achievement: Through my relation with Others and my new experiences, I become more capable of self-correction and self-continuation.
