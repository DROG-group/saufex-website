As flatlanders from within a lower dimension, we would not be able to establish whether anything we encounter is merely part of our existing relational organization or might constitute the “zero” through which a higher dimension could become possible. Without such a zero, there is no higher level accessible to us from which we could reflect on our current dimension. As flatlanders, we therefore would not be able to establish the “right” worth of what or whom we encounter, nor establish that no higher dimension exists.\
Dogmatism claims to possess the zero; relativism denies that there is one. Both exceed what can be established from within the flatland. A defensible response, if we are flatlanders, is instead to preserve ma: the conditions under which alternative relational organizations can encounter one another without being prematurely assimilated, evaluated, or declared incommensurable.
