Let's assume we are flatlanders.\
As flatlanders from within a lower dimension, we would not be able to establish whether anything we encounter is merely part of our existing relational organization or might constitute a “zero”, a hypothetical condition of exteriority through which a higher dimension could become possible. Without access to such a zero, we, as flatlanders, would not be able to establish the “right” worth of what or whom we encounter, nor establish that no higher dimension exists.\
Dogmatism claims to possess the zero; relativism denies that there is one. Both exceed what can be established from within the flatland. A defensible response, if we are flatlanders, is instead to preserve ma: the conditions under which alternative relational organizations can encounter one another without being prematurely assimilated, evaluated, or declared incommensurable.
