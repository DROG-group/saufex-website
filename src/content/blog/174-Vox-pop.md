## Introduction
A third didactic mechanism to understand external ontologies beyond listening without immediate reaction or judgment is what we call in our book ([e-book](https://jumpshare.com/share/l9PdhpCFTOq2KePRFJgP); [pdf]( https://interdemocracy.wordpress.com/interdemocracy-english-pdf/)) “vox pop”.\
Vox pop allows students to encounter the personal ontologies of a group of adolescents extending far beyond their own classroom. What distinguishes this instrument is that students are not merely exposed to these personal ontologies; they also have to assume ethical responsibility for representing them. This requires them to represent these perspectives without colouring them through their own personal ontologies. It marks a significant step beyond merely listening without immediate reaction or judgment: students must move from receiving external perspectives to faithfully conveying them.

## Vox pop
From our book:\
„The element “vox pop” gives a voice to a larger group of adolescents beyond their current configuration. Beata and I use vox pop to unearth the needs and desires of different segments of adolescents (e.g., students of all first classes) and present the resulting collective portrait to a specific target group, such as teachers or parents.\
The first step in this process is to, together with the adolescents, identify and specify a current challenge. That challenge is then collectively phrased. This can be done through a [thought experiment] or citizen-to-citizen dialogue initially, followed by a vote to decide which outcomes to further pursue.\
Once the challenge is phrased in the form of a question, the second step is to prepare the adolescents to visit other groups and request the adolescents in these groups anonymously respond in writing. Representatives of the adolescents of the initial group should ask for permission to interrupt the ongoing sessions of the other groups. They can then introduce the question and its aim, which is to create a collective portrait about the related challenge. Subsequently, the adolescent representatives distribute sheets of paper and initiate a 90-second response period. Finally, they collect the responses and thank both the adolescents and adult running the session. \
The third step is to transform the initial group of adolescents into a group of editors. These editors should gather all the provided responses into one place and filter out overlapping answers and overly frivolous answers. This should be supplemented with serious discussions that touch on interpretation and freedom of speech. The responses that remain after the filtering process should be edited for clarity only.\
Step four is to digitalize all edited answers. The answers are randomly distributed among all the editors, who read these answers out loud while being recorded. The recorded answers are then edited into one piece. Finally, the answers are put on individual text boards synchronized with the audio. The result is a video representing a collective portrait of the challenge.”
