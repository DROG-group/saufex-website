## Introduction
Human beings do not encounter information through an empty cognitive channel. Incoming information is interpreted through a personal ontology: a relatively stabilised structure of lived experience, prior interpretations and reinterpretations of reality, expectations, identity, belonging, achievement, prior knowledge, and institutional trust. A personal ontology is necessary; without an organisation of one’s interpretations, meaningful action would not be possible. Yet such an organisation is necessarily incomplete: no individual has, for example, experienced everything or belonged to every social environment. This creates *interpretive distortion*: whether information encountered is accurate or inaccurate, the personal ontology through which it is interpreted may systematically exclude relevant dimensions of reality. Interpretive distortion therefore requires intervention at two levels. DROG addresses it cognitively through EMOD and behaviorally through Interdemocracy, both drawing on the findings of the HORIZON-funded European project Saufex.
## EMOD
EMOD is a publication by DROG for professionals who need to understand how disinformation, influence, and social tensions affect their work. It is designed for professionals working in public administration, policy, communications, public safety, and other public-facing services where competing narratives and declining trust can have practical consequences. EMOD is organised into annual editions, each bringing together thematic dossiers around issues relevant to a particular professional audience. The dossiers connect documented cases and research with different perspectives and interactive explorations. Readers examine how stories gain credibility, why people participate, how participation becomes influence or pressure, and what they may expect their own responses to change.\
DROG contributes more than information. Its expertise, frameworks and approach to interactive learning help readers recognise patterns, question assumptions and understand situations from perspectives they might otherwise miss. The experience respects professional expertise: it supports judgment rather than prescribing how professionals should do their job. Alongside the dossiers, a reusable toolbox provides frameworks and practical ways to investigate a situation. Readers can explore explanations at different depths and save insights together with their supporting sources. These materials remain useful across themes and annual editions.\
Access is offered through an organisation-wide subscription to an annual edition, covering all staff rather than individual seats or courses. Each new edition provides a fresh collection of relevant issues, cases and perspectives, while the shared explanations and tools support continued use in everyday professional practice.
## Interdemocracy
Interdemocracy is a method and format for encountering the personal ontologies of others without immediately closing the encounter through judgment, evaluation, persuasion, acceptance, or rejection. In a workshop setting, participants experience formulating their own perspectives without anticipating an immediate reaction from others, and listen to others without reacting immediately. This creates leeway for perspectives to be encountered without requiring an immediate response or incorporation into one’s own personal ontology. Through Interdemocracy, professionals and members of the public can encounter perspectives that might otherwise be excluded by their interpretive distortion.
