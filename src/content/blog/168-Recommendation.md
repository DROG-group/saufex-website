## Enhancing societal resilience needs to be the focus for all-of-society involvement in decision-making regarding information manipulation
Decision-making regarding information manipulation requires more than decentralization, including the participation of independent non-governmental experts. It also requires democratization through meaningful involvement of the general public. These two dimensions are complementary: decentralization preserves independent sources of knowledge and perspective outside government institutions, while democratization ensures that citizens' perspectives and situated knowledge can become consequential for collective decision-making. To maximize the positive impact on societal resilience, participation and deliberation processes should be designed to prevent premature assimilation of different perspectives into dominant institutional or group interpretations. They should preserve participants' autonomy while enabling encounters with perspectives that challenge their existing assumptions. In particular, participation and reception methods should strengthen participants' experiences of autonomy, belonging and achievement, while safeguarding the conditions necessary for meaningful and safe participation. The overarching objective is to strengthen societal resilience by enhancing society's capacity for independent judgment, mutual recognition and collective self-correction, while improving the quality and legitimacy of decisions regarding information manipulation.
