## Introduction
A second didactic mechanism to support students from going from step one (to listen without immediate reaction or judgment) to step two (avoiding premature assimilation of an external personal ontology) - see: [blog post 171](https://saufex.eu/insights/171-Fundamental-questions-regarding-human-ontologies/) - is what we call in our book Interdemocracy ([e-book](https://jumpshare.com/share/l9PdhpCFTOq2KePRFJgP); [pdf](https://interdemocracy.wordpress.com/interdemocracy-english-pdf/)): letter from a parent or guardian. During this element students encounter the personal ontology of a parent of one of the students. This extends the scope of both the basic format and the citizen-to-citizen mechanism (see: [blog post 172](https://saufex.eu/insights/172-Citizen-to-citizen/)) to include an intergenerational dimension.

## Letter from a parent or guardian
From our book:\
“An alternative way to execute the basic form of the program is to invite a parent/guardian to the group. This changes both the dynamic and the structure of the session. The dynamic is altered by the presence of two adults facilitating the session, and ideally, this will allow adolescents to observe and find inspiration in the communication between the adults. For the facilitator, this requires a clear understanding with the parent/guardian about responsibilities (i.e., the facilitator is ultimately responsible), roles (i.e., the parent/guardian may not act as just a passive, critical observer), and modes of communication (i.e., it is advisable to implement this through citizen-to-citizen guidelines). \
With a parent/guardian present, the format changes in several ways. First, the introduction should consist of the parent/guardian reading a letter they have written to the class. The letter must concern a topic relevant for all adolescents present, not just for the child of the parent/guardian, and must be addressed to the entire group. The letter should be experience-based and not claim to be evidence-based; it can also contain a question that will function as the point of reference for the dialogues that follow.\ 
After the parent/guardian has read their letter and the facilitator has thanked them for it, the facilitator should ask the adolescents to write their answers to the parent/guardian’s question or to write a question to the parent/ guardian (in the case that the parent/guardian did not provide a question). /…/\
Next, the facilitator randomly chooses an adolescent to stand up and read what they have written /…/. The parent/guardian then reacts. Ideally, all adolescents should be selected. After the dialogue, the facilitator proceeds with the check-out, including the parent/guardian in the process.\
The element “letter from a parent/guardian” adds reciprocal communication between an adult (i.e., the parent/guardian) and an adolescent based on experience.”
